On a server with a multi-tenant licence, whoever runs the server (the instance operator, often your managed security provider) cannot look inside your tenant unless you allow it. You allow it with **Support access**. This page is for tenant administrators: it explains the three levels, how to change them, and how to see what the operator did.

## Who can set support access

- You need the **Administrator** role in your own tenant, signed in on your tenant's own address.
- The page exists only on a multi-tenant licence. It is not shown to the instance operator, and the operator cannot change it for you.
- It cannot be changed for a deactivated tenant.

## The levels

| Level | Description shown on the page | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **No access** (default) | "The operator sees that your tenant exists and nothing else. Requests into your data are refused." | The operator sees your tenant's name, domain and status on their **Tenants** page, but no counts and no data, and cannot enter your tenant. |
| **Read only** | "The operator can look at your alerts, logs and configuration to help you. They cannot change anything — every write is refused, whatever their own role says." | The operator can open your pages and settings. Every change is refused. In UTMStack v12.0.2 the alert list, Log Explorer, dashboard charts and the SOC-AI assistant also do not work in a read-only session (see **Working Inside a Tenant**). |
| **Full access** | "The operator can act inside your tenant as an administrator: change configuration, users and response flows." | The operator works with Administrator rights: they can change settings, users, integrations, rules and flows. Their changes are real. |

With **Read only** or **Full access**, the operator's **Tenants** page also shows your number of users, data sources, open alerts and AI requests used today.

## Change the level

<Steps>

<Step title="Open Support access">

Go to **Configure > Settings > Support access**.

</Step>

<Step title="Choose a level">

Click **No access**, **Read only** or **Full access**. The level in force is marked **current**. When you choose **Full access**, the page warns: "Full access lets the operator change things on your behalf. Grant it while you need hands-on help, and drop back afterwards."

</Step>

<Step title="Save">

Click **Save**. You see "Support access updated", or "Support access revoked" when you choose **No access**.

</Step>

</Steps>

The new level applies to the operator's very next request, even while they are working inside your tenant. Your change is recorded in your audit log as `tenant.support_access.set`.

<Tip>

Grant **Full access** only for the time you need hands-on help, for example during onboarding, and go back to **Read only** or **No access** afterwards.

</Tip>

## See what the operator did

"Everything the operator does here is recorded in your audit log." Each change the operator makes inside your tenant appears in **Configure > Settings > App Audit Logs** with the operator's email address as the user. Use the **User** filter to list only their actions. See **App Audit Logs**.

## What the instance operator cannot do, whatever you choose

- Change your support access level.
- Sign in on your tenant's address with their own account: their account exists only in their own tenant.

## Messages

| Message | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Only an administrator of this tenant can change this" | You are not an Administrator of this tenant. |
| "Could not read the current setting." | The page could not load the level. Click **Retry**. |
| "Could not save the change" | The change was not saved. Try again. |
