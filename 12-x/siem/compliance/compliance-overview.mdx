**Compliance** shows how well your environment meets security frameworks such as PCI DSS, ISO/IEC 27001 or GDPR. UTMStack v12 proves what it can from your logs and alerts, and lets a person record a verdict on everything else. This page explains how frameworks, requirements and controls fit together, and how statuses and the score are worked out.

## Who can use Compliance

**Compliance** is in the main menu, so every user sees it.

| Action | Permission | Built-in roles that have it |
|---|---|---|
| Open frameworks, controls and reports, download PDFs | `compliance.read` | Administrator, Analyst, Viewer |
| Run evaluations, record verdicts and notes, create and change frameworks, controls and schedules | `compliance.write` | Administrator |

The edit buttons are shown to every user, but UTMStack refuses the change for a user without `compliance.write`. See **Roles and Permissions** to give it to a custom role.

Frameworks you create, reports, verdicts, notes and schedules belong to a tenant. Each tenant has its own.

## The Compliance page

Click **Compliance** in the main menu. The page has three tabs:

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Frameworks** | One card per framework, with its score (or "Not evaluated" until it has run once) and how many controls it uses. Click a card to open its report. See **Compliance Reports**. |
| **Controls** | The control library. Search with **Search controls…** or pick a family in **All families**. Up to 200 controls are listed at a time; refine the search to see others. Click a control to read it. |
| **Schedule** | Reports that run and are emailed on a schedule. See **Scheduled Compliance Reports**. |

## How frameworks, requirements and controls fit together

```text
framework  →  section                          →  requirement  →  controls
PCI DSS       Requirement 10: Log and monitor …    10.2.1.4        ac-7
```

- A **framework** is a standard or regulation, for example PCI DSS v4.0.1.
- It is split into **sections** (its own chapters) and **requirements**, each with the framework's official id, for example `10.2.1.4` or `Art.32`.
- Each requirement points to one or more **controls** that satisfy it.
- The controls come from one shared library. A control is defined once and used by every framework that needs it: control `ac-7` (Unsuccessful Logon Attempts) answers PCI DSS 10.2.1.4, CMMC AC.L2-3.1.8, NIST SP 800-171 03.01.08 and ISO A.8.5 alike.

The library is the NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5 catalogue (a public catalogue of security controls from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology): 320 controls. Control ids look like `au-6`; a few control enhancements look like `ia-2.1`.

## Automatic and manual controls

| Kind | In the control file | How it is judged | In the shipped library |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Automatic** | `scope: data` | By its checks, which count matching logs or alerts in the reporting window | 45 controls with 795 checks |
| **Manual** | `scope: governance` | By a person. Logs cannot show policies, training, physical security or settings such as password rules. | 275 controls |

Checks come in two kinds:

- **Evidence checks** show that something is in place, for example "failed logons are recorded". They pass when at least a minimum number of records match (643 shipped checks).
- **Limit checks** count things that must not happen, for example "an audit log was cleared" or "a sign-in without multi-factor authentication". They fail when more than the allowed number of records match, usually more than 0 (152 shipped checks).

Every check reads one data type, such as `wineventlog` or `o365`. A check for a source your tenant does not send neither passes nor fails.

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Compliant** | The control's checks passed, or a person recorded it as compliant. |
| **Non-compliant** | A check failed, or a limit was exceeded. |
| **At risk** | Some checks passed, but others could not run (strategy ALL only). |
| **Not covered** | Nothing measures this: the control has no check, or it is not in the library. |
| **Not evaluated** | The control has checks, but your tenant sent no data of the kind they read in the reporting window. |
| **Pending** | A check is declared but not written yet, or has no rule. |
| **Out of scope** | A manual control that no person has judged yet. |

### How a control gets its status

1. A **manual** control is **Out of scope** until a person sets its status.
2. For an **automatic** control, UTMStack runs each check over the reporting window:
   - A check whose data type has no records in the window is **No data**. It neither passes nor fails.
   - An evidence check passes when the number of matching records is at least its minimum (usually 1).
   - A limit check passes when the number of matching records is at most its maximum (usually 0).
3. Then:
   - If every check had no data, the control is **Not evaluated**.
   - If any limit check failed, the control is **Non-compliant**, whatever its strategy. A source that logs correctly does not hide a violation on another source.
   - With strategy **ANY**, one passing evidence check is enough for **Compliant**.
   - With strategy **ALL**, any failed check makes it **Non-compliant**. If the others passed but some could not run, it is **At risk**.

A person's verdict always replaces the engine's result on the report. See **Compliance Reports**.

### How a requirement gets its status

A requirement takes the worst status among its controls, in this order from worst to best:

**Non-compliant**, **Not covered**, **At risk**, **Compliant**, **Not evaluated**, **Pending**, **Out of scope**.

A requirement with no controls is **Not covered**.

## How the score is counted

The score is the share of the framework's requirements that are **Compliant**:

> score = compliant requirements × 100 ÷ all requirements in the framework

The report page says it in words, for example "40 of 70 requirements met · 120 in the framework": 40 requirements are compliant, 70 could be judged, and the framework has 120.

Every requirement counts, including the ones nobody could judge. So:

- Requirements that need a person's verdict (manual controls) keep the score down until someone records a status.
- A source that stops sending logs makes requirements **Not evaluated** and lowers the score too.

Use the **Score over time** chart on the report to tell the two apart: it shows the score and the share of requirements that could be judged. See **Compliance Reports**.

## Coverage and activity

Each control in a report also shows **Coverage** (how many enabled correlation rules are linked to the control) and **Activity** (how many alerts those rules raised in the window). In UTMStack v12.0.2 no rule is linked to a control, so both show 0 rules and 0 alerts. Judge controls by their checks.
